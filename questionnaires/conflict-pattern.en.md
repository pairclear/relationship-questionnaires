# One fight or a pattern? How we argue

[Take it online (runs in your browser, sends nothing)](https://pairclear.github.io/relationship-questionnaires/?q=conflict-pattern&lang=en)

> Self-reflection, not diagnosis. Written by PairClear, not validated in research. It measures how things go between two people, not anyone's psyche.

About how arguments go between you: whether the same fight repeats, how it escalates, and whether you find a way back. Two questions are about safety; if either applies, the result will show where to get help.

**Think about:** the last 3 months.

**Answer scale:** 0 = Never, 1 = Rarely, 2 = Sometimes, 3 = Often, 4 = Almost always

| # | Statement | Part |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | We argue about the same topic again and again. | Repeating loop |
| 2 | I can predict how an argument will go from its first sentence. | Repeating loop |
| 3 | Old arguments are brought into new ones. | Repeating loop |
| 4 | Arguments turn into criticism of who the other person is, not what they did. | Escalation |
| 5 | One of us shouts, insults or mocks the other. | Escalation |
| 6 | One of us shuts down or leaves and won't talk for a long time. | Escalation |
| 7 | After an argument, one of us makes a step towards the other. | Repair |
| 8 | We can later talk calmly about what happened. | Repair |
| 9 | In the middle of an argument, one of us can soften it (a joke, a touch, 'let's start over') and the other accepts it. | Repair |
| 10 | I feel afraid of my partner during or after arguments. | *safety question, not scored* |
| 11 | My partner controls my money, phone or who I can see, or threatens me. | *safety question, not scored* |

## Scoring

Reverse the marked items (4 becomes 0, 3 becomes 1). Average the items of each part to get a 0-4 score. Under 1.5 is low, 1.5 to 2.5 is in the middle, above 2.5 is high. These are not norms or cut-offs: the questionnaire is not validated. Use the result to start a conversation, not to judge.

## Parts

**Repeating loop**

- low: Arguments don't seem to run in one loop.
- mid: Some arguments repeat. Try naming the loop together, as 'us against the loop', not 'me against you'.
- high: The same argument keeps coming back. That usually means the topic underneath hasn't been heard yet. A couples counsellor can help you get out of the loop.

**Escalation**

- low: Arguments rarely get out of hand.
- mid: Sometimes it gets heated. Agree in a calm moment on a pause signal and how long the pause lasts.
- high: Arguments often get heated or personal. A pause rule helps; if it doesn't, it's worth getting outside help.

**Repair**

- low: It's hard to find a way back after an argument. Small repair attempts (a joke, a touch, 'let's start over') count.
- mid: You sometimes find a way back. Notice what worked last time.
- high: You usually find your way back. That's a real strength.

> If you are afraid of your partner, are being threatened or controlled, or are thinking about hurting yourself: call emergency services (112 in Europe, 911 in the US and Canada) or find a free helpline at https://findahelpline.com.

## Questions to think about

- What is the argument really about, underneath the topic?
- What do you need from your partner at the moment it starts to escalate?
- What helped you make up the last time it worked?
