# Who keeps track of everything: the mental load at home

[Take it online (runs in your browser, sends nothing)](https://pairclear.github.io/relationship-questionnaires/?q=mental-load&lang=en)

> Self-reflection, not diagnosis. Written by PairClear, not validated in research. It measures how things go between two people, not anyone's psyche.

Housework is not just doing, it's also noticing, planning and reminding. These questions are about your share of each. Each partner can fill it in separately and compare.

**Think about:** a typical recent month.

**Answer scale:** 0 = Never, 1 = Rarely, 2 = Sometimes, 3 = Often, 4 = Almost always

## Scoring

Reverse the marked items (4 becomes 0, 3 becomes 1). Average the items of each part to get a 0-4 score. Under 1.5 is low, 1.5 to 2.5 is in the middle, above 2.5 is high. These are not norms or cut-offs: the questionnaire is not validated. Use the result to start a conversation, not to judge.

## Parts

**My share of the invisible work**

- low: The invisible work doesn't fall mainly on you.
- mid: You carry a good part of it. Make a list together of what needs noticing, not just doing.
- high: Most of the noticing and planning is on you. A fair split is about handing over whole areas (from noticing to doing), not single tasks.

**Can we talk about it**

- low: It's hard to raise this without a fight. Try a calm time and one concrete area, not 'you never help'.
- mid: Sometimes you can talk about it. What made those times work?
- high: You can talk about it. That makes changing the split much easier.

## Questions to think about

- Which one area would you hand over completely, from noticing to doing?
- What do you need to let go of for that to work (for example, it being done your way)?
- How would you raise it in one sentence, without 'always' and 'never'?
